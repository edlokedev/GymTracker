# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People tracking strength, cardio, and timed workouts on a phone, often between sets with limited attention and tired hands. Their core jobs are fast workout capture, quick comparison with prior performance, and confident review of whether training is progressing.

## Product Purpose

Gymmie makes personal workout history useful. Logging should stay fast enough for the gym floor; progress views should turn recorded sets into truthful, comparable signals. Success means users can answer “What improved, what stalled, and what should I do next?” without interpreting a dense analytics dashboard.

## Brand Personality

Focused, motivating, no-nonsense. Calm confidence over hype. Encouragement comes from clear evidence of progress, not decoration or gamified pressure.

## Anti-references

- Enterprise BI dashboards with dense controls, rainbow legends, and overlapping series.
- Social fitness feeds that prioritize comparison, streak pressure, or spectacle over personal training.
- Decorative “athletic” interfaces with neon gradients, aggressive motion, or oversized marketing metrics.
- Charts that imply precision while mixing incompatible exercises, units, or missing data.

## Design Principles

1. Put the gym-floor task first: primary actions and insights must work one-handed on a phone.
2. Compare like with like: one exercise, one metric, one clear time range.
3. Make data honest: missing values stay missing; aggregation rules remain predictable.
4. Lead with the answer: show latest performance, meaningful change, and records before detailed history.
5. Reward consistency without pressure: use calm, evidence-based feedback instead of guilt or spectacle.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA. Interactive targets are at least 44px. Meaning never depends on color alone. Charts include text summaries and accessible labels. Respect reduced-motion preferences. Keep controls legible and usable during movement, glare, and low-light gym conditions.
